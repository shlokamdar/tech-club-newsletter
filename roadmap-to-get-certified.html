Beyond the
Certificate

What Harsh, Riya and Naman shared about certifications, exam realities, practical learning and building a career beyond the badge.

Certifications are everywhere. They sit on LinkedIn profiles, appear in job descriptions and can feel like the fastest way to prove that you know your stuff.

But during the Tech Club's certification panel, the conversation got a little more honest.

Harsh, Riya and Naman shared their experiences with cybersecurity, cloud and AI certifications, including how they prepared, what the exams actually looked like and where certifications fit into a larger career path.

01 / The Certifications

From ethical hacking to cloud and AI

The discussion covered a wide range of certifications, including Certified Ethical Hacker, penetration testing, Security+, Microsoft Azure AI certifications, AWS Solutions Architect, AI security and risk, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure and social engineering defence.

Rather than treating certifications as interchangeable badges, the panel connected them to different areas of technology and different stages of learning.

02 / What the exams actually look like

Proctored, timed and very real.

The panel gave students a glimpse into what certification exams can actually look like once you are sitting on the other side of the preparation.

For the CEH exam discussed during the session, the examination lasts approximately six hours. Candidates are proctored throughout the exam, with their examination environment and screen monitored.

Before beginning, candidates may need to show their examination room to the proctor. During the exam, even stepping away for something as simple as getting water needs to be communicated.

The panel also discussed the CPENT exam, which runs for 24 hours and is divided into two 12-hour slots. It is a very different kind of commitment from a conventional certification exam and highlights just how intensive some practical cybersecurity assessments can be.

The takeaway is simple: before booking an exam, understand the format, duration and conditions. Certification preparation is not just about knowing the syllabus. You also need to know what the exam experience itself demands.

And one warning was very clear.

Don't use services that promise to take the exam for you or guarantee a pass. The panel shared that candidates caught using such services can face certification bans and lose future opportunities.

03 / Look for opportunities

Not every certification needs your wallet.

One of the useful takeaways from the session was that students should look for opportunities before reaching for their cards.

The panel discussed free or voucher-based opportunities, including certifications obtained through LinkedIn promotions and merit-based university pathways.

One panelist had received a certification opportunity through an elective course after performing well. The certification could replace a project requirement, making it useful both academically and professionally.

04 / The Real Preparation

Fundamentals first. Practice second. Certificate third.

Across the discussion, the panel kept returning to fundamentals. Knowing the concepts makes both theoretical and practical questions easier to approach.

Practice tests can reveal where you are weak. Labs can show you how the concepts work. Troubleshooting forces you to understand why something works instead of simply remembering that it does.

For cybersecurity students, practical platforms, CTFs and hands-on exercises were highlighted as useful ways to build that experience.

05 / Find your direction

Pick a domain before picking a badge.

The panel encouraged students to first understand which area they want to explore. Once you have a direction, certifications become easier to choose.

06 / Timing Matters

Don't wait until the final trimester.

The panel suggested starting early rather than leaving certifications and portfolio building until the very end of the course.

Doing certifications alongside coursework can also create a useful cycle: learn the concept, practise it, then apply it. By the time resumes begin circulating, you have more than a list of certifications. You have experience behind them.

07 / What Comes After

Build something.

The panel also encouraged students to participate in CTFs, hackathons and practical projects.

A certification can demonstrate that you completed an assessment. A project demonstrates that you can take knowledge and turn it into something real.

A Future-Facing Takeaway

Learn beyond the exam.

The discussion around AI highlighted another important point: understanding the fundamentals is only the beginning. Learning how models work, how to build with them and how to deploy them can turn theoretical knowledge into practical capability.

Stuck on what to choose?

Talk to your faculty.

The panel highlighted faculty guidance as a useful resource when students are unsure about a domain, certification or career direction. The advice is available, but students need to ask for it.

The Takeaway

Build capability,
not just a checklist.

The session started with a simple question: which certifications should students pursue?

It ended with a much better one: what do you actually want to learn, and can you prove that you know it?

Pick a domain. Build your fundamentals. Practise. Ask questions. Work on projects. Then choose the certifications that support that journey.

A certificate can open a door.
Your skills are what carry you through it.
